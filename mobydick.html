Moby Dick

Very long book, so I will be doing a chapter by chapter review as I read it.


Chapter 1: Loomings.
Call me Ishmael. Hard opening line, followed by multiple pages of philosophical lollygaggery. I get what people say now when I am told that Melville has the most beautiful prose in the English language.
I had to do a lot of googling to get historical context for some of the named locations and terms, worth it. There was some truly beautiful phrasing and sentences in this chapter, here are a few.

"With a philosophical flourish Cato throws himself upon his sword; I quietly take to the ship."

"Let the most absent-minded of men be plunged in his deepest reveries—stand that man on his legs, set his feet a-going, and he will infallibly lead you to water, if water there be in all that region.
Should you ever be athirst in the great American desert, try this experiment, if your caravan happen to be supplied with a metaphysical professor."
Were the Niagara but a cataract of sand, would you travel your thousand miles to see it?"

"And still deeper the meaning of that story of Narcissus, who becuase hec ould not grasp the tormenting, mild image he saw in the fountain, plunged into it and was drowned. But that same image, we ourselves
see in all rivers and oceans. It is the image of the ungraspable phantom of life; and this is the key to it all."

"No, when I go to sea, I go as a simple sailor, right before the mast, plumb down into the forecastle, aloft the to the royal mast-head."

"Grand Contested Election for the Presidency of the United States"
"WHALING VOYAGE BY ONE ISHMAEL"
"BLOODY BATTLE IN AFFGHANISTAN"

Chapter 2: The Carpet-Bag.
Such dreary streets! I now realize that there are levels to this. The dogged stubborness to go on a Nantucket craft, for there was the honour and history, as opposed to "better" modern alternatives is one
that appeals to many. I am glad I understood the line "The Tyre of this Carthage," which I would not have if I hadn't watched that documentary on the origins of Carthage just a few days before. There is a
spirit to Nantucket that isn't there in New Bedford, brought if only by its history of whaling. "
