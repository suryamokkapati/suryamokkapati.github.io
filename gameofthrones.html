A Game of Thrones

Not particularly classic literature, but definitely a classic.
I am a huge fan of the show, and of the overwhelming opinion that some of the changes that the writers made in the latter seasons were up there with murder as far as sins go.

It is interesting how flawed we can see Ned Stark in the book compared to the HBO show. In the show he was a stoic figure who rarely had moments of weakness, however in the novel we can see
his jealousy of his older brother Brandon, the bitterness he holds to Baelish and Varys, and the real weight of the betrayal of his honour at the end.

Catelyn is far more unlikeable in the books, she competes with Cersei and wins as my least favourite so far. The grieving of a mother is one thing, to tell another child that it should have been them is a whole different ball game.
She practically killed her husband by taking the imp (who, coincidentally is my favourite character so far), she acts irrationally and emotionally, and will get Robb killed by the Freys.

I really enjoyed this book, and it gave a whole lot more insight to the world of Westeros and Essos, which is just a masterfully crafted tapestry.
