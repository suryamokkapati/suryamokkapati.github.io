The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde

Very interesting book that tackles some pretty cool projects. Obvious disclaimer that the plot will be discussed.

The separation of the binary good and evil in a man is an interesting enough concept in its own right, but the idea that evil can be extracted and the remaining person would still be caught in the struggle between good and evil, implying that the base form of humanity tends towards evil is interesting to see.

I read this book completely before I was told that it could be interpreted through the lens of homosexuality. This opens up a whole new can of worms and I damn near reread the entire book because it made so much sense.
Wasn't it Saint Paul that wrote "I do not the good that I want, but but the evil I do not want is what I keep doing?"
The witness of the "murder" could be a witness to acts of homosexuality, at the time outlawed in England, and it would be an interesting parallel to draw. That as well as the fact that he tried to split himself, maybe depravity at night and church on Sunday,
and yet in the end depravity won. It is a bleak story, but still interesting.

There is also a Nietzschean interpretation of this, which was what I originally thought was going on, and it still may be the case. Dr. Jekyll's virtue seems to stem from great social stigma and pressure, completely ripped away at the
autonomy of Hyde. Jekyll is shackled by the rules, customs, laws, and emotional sentiment of his time, and Hyde is none of those things and more. He floats across the tightrope, not needing to care or to feel but hate.
There is an irony there, that only Jekyll could walk in the sunlight, and yet Hyde was free. Do humans truly want authentic freedom? Is Hyde what would lie in wait if we did? As Stevenson himself put it, "If he shall be Mr. Hyde, I shall become Mr. Seek."
